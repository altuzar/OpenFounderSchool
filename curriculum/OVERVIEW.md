# An original curriculum for after school

The program unit is a **six-hour week**: three 90-minute blocks and one 90-minute review. Each year has 40 active weeks, with breaks scheduled by the family. Four years total 160 weeks and 960 hours, excluding vacations or additional commercial work. Additional work is not required to pass.

The design fits entrepreneurial learning around school life. It does not move a full school day into the afternoon or replace school subjects, exams, or accreditation. You can spread a unit across two weeks; week numbers indicate learning order, not mandatory calendar dates.

## Three project paths

- **Small service:** help with a nonsensitive digital task. Deliver manually before automating. Example: organize a fictional catalog for a family shop.
- **Digital product:** a local tool for a bounded task. Example: a tournament organizer using invented data. Publication or payment is not required.
- **Knowledge:** an original guide or material someone can use. Example: a tutorial fixing a problem in a safe hobby. Do not copy books or promise medical or financial outcomes.

Paths share the same curriculum. Students unable to work with real customers use labeled simulation. Never replace an exercise with invented information presented as reality.

## Weekly rhythm

1. **Understand and practice (90 minutes):** 10 for context, 20 reading/discussion, 20 for an independent attempt, 10 for a break, 25 practice, 5 recording.
2. **Build (90 minutes):** 10 for scope, 30 independent work, 10 for a break, 30 assisted work, 10 checking.
3. **Test (90 minutes):** 15 for preparation/permissions, 30 testing, 10 for a break, 25 analysis, 10 evidence.
4. **Review (90 minutes):** use the parent handbook and rubric. Presentation, numbers, and the next decision, with breaks as needed.

## Skills developed throughout

Listening, explaining, distinguishing facts from hypotheses, testing ideas, building something bounded, measuring costs, collaborating, recognizing risks, and using AI with judgment. Demonstrate competence through explanations and evidence. No AI score defines a student's ability or personal worth.

## Reading, writing, and sources

Use the [original readings](READINGS.md) and revisit them with different examples. A weekly reflection can be 250–500 words, three minutes of audio, an explained diagram, or an equivalent demonstration. Publication is not required. [Reference videos](../research/youtube-notes.md) are optional; losing access never blocks a lesson. Obtain commercial books legally if the family chooses to read them; no purchase is required.

## Progression criteria

Each week has a specific artifact and criterion. Years 2–4 develop four-week projects: investigate, design, test, and decide. [ASSESSMENT](../docs/ASSESSMENT.md) defines human review. Financial goals are separate from graduation and never become individual quotas.
