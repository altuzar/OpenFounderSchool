# Demo and defense

1. Who has the problem and what evidence supports it.
2. What we built and a demo of the minimum journey.
3. What happened in testing: results, rejections, and limitations.
4. Numbers: revenue, costs, provisional profit, currency, period, and support.
5. Student contribution, AI contribution, and adult review.
6. What failed, what changed, and why.
7. Risks, next experiment, and specific request for feedback.

Never present simulated figures as real traction or goals as results. Rehearse
an uncomfortable question about your main assumption and answer without inventing.
